## 4 MLP: verifying the four stages of the mechanism in order

**〔4-0〕§3の四段を、一隠れ層（4.1）と二隠れ層のMLP（4.2〜4.4）で順に確かめる。**

We verify the four stages of Section 3 in order, in a one-hidden-layer MLP (Section 4.1) and in two-hidden-layer MLPs (Sections 4.2 to 4.4).

### 4.1 The push is created by learning and rebuilt at every task

**〔4.1-1〕一隠れ層MNISTの5活性化（§3.3の5つ、各2 seed）で、§3.1の押しが学習で作られ、課題ごとに作り直され、実際にユニットを沈めることを確かめた。**

In one-hidden-layer MNIST with the five activations of Section 3.3 (two seeds each), we confirmed that the push of Section 3.1 is created by learning, is rebuilt at every task, and actually sinks the units. The expected push at the switch rose as a nearly constant multiple of $\log C-L$, the amount by which the task-1 loss had fallen below chance, with no threshold-like step (quartiles of the ratio after 50% training accuracy: 0.18 to 0.22). In a post-hoc analysis over 30 tasks, $\kappa$ at the end of each task never fell below 0.83 times its value at the end of task 1, and from task 2 on, 74 to 95% of $\kappa$ was rebuilt by the change of the features. In the first 200 updates after a switch (Adam), 82 to 100% of the living units actually sank (range of the values over task intervals). Except for leaky ReLU, the self-form agreed with the expected push in direction in 90 to 100% of cases, but it carried only 13 to 60% of the magnitude (Appendix D). Returns within a task occurred inside the negative side: of 1,000 units that had once entered $p<1/2$, only 4 were back at $p\ge1/2$ at the end of a later task.

### 4.2 Blocking the pathways: centering and bias suppression

**〔4.2-1〕§3.2の二つの経路を別々に塞ぐ。二隠れ層の乱数ラベルCIFAR・raw・ReLUで、入力平均を引くC、両隠れ層の出力平均を引くH、両者を組み合わせたCHを比較した。**

We block the two pathways of Section 3.2 separately. In two-hidden-layer random-label CIFAR with raw inputs and ReLU, we compared three operations: C subtracts the input mean, H subtracts the output mean of both hidden layers, and CH combines the two. With C alone the second layer was lost, and with H alone the first layer was lost; in each case the lost layer was the one whose input-mean pathway stayed open. The online accuracy over tasks 31 to 50 was 11.33% for C and 98.67% for CH. CHB, which additionally suppresses the bias, reached 98.54%. CHB adds two operations to CH from task 1 on: the first-layer bias is zeroed after every update, and decoupled weight decay is applied to the second-layer bias.

**〔4.2-2〕別の10 seed（10–19）で200課題まで調べると、課題181–200のonline精度は、CHが62.98%、CHBが99.03%だった。**

Another 10 seeds (10 to 19) were run to 200 tasks. The online accuracy over tasks 181 to 200 was 62.98% for CH and 99.03% for CHB; CH declined in all 10 seeds, while CHB kept its high accuracy. In the first layer with centered inputs, the common position over the training images coincides with the bias ($m_1=b_1$), and under CH it sank to the negative side. As Section 3.2 states, blocking the weight pathway by centering leaves the push in the bias pathway, and long-term relearning was preserved only when that pathway was blocked as well.

### 4.3 Stopping the width: growth caps

**〔4.3-1〕§3.3では、幅が自己形の零点を深める活性化（ELU）と、生きた零点の無い活性化（ReLU）があった。**

Section 3.3 distinguished an activation whose self-form zero is deepened by the width (ELU) from one that has no zero in a living state (ReLU). We placed a cap on the growth of the weights to stop the width from growing, and asked whether this difference appears in relearning. The radius of each unit is the row norm of its input weights at the end of task 1; from task 2 on, after every Adam update, a row that exceeds its radius is projected back onto it (cap12 for both layers, cap1 and cap2 for one layer).

**〔4.3-2〕乱数ラベルCIFAR・std・ELUでは、課題31–50のonline精度が対照10.09%に対しcap12で85.98%となり、全10 seedで崩壊を防いだ。**

In random-label CIFAR with standardized inputs and ELU, the online accuracy over tasks 31 to 50 was 85.98% under cap12 against 10.09% for the control, and the collapse was prevented in all 10 seeds. Capping one layer was not enough (cap1 11.91%, cap2 25.10%). The norm of the second-layer input mean grew from 52.6 to 1157 in the control between tasks 1 and 50, and stayed at 40 under cap12 (seed average).

**〔4.3-3〕同じ乱数ラベルCIFARのReLUは、対照が床（11.3%）にあり、cap12でも変わらなかった。**

For ReLU in the same random-label CIFAR, the control was at the floor (11.3%), and cap12 did not change it. For ReLU in real-label 5+1 CIFAR, cap12 lowered the late online accuracy by 3.6 points [97.5% confidence interval $-6.7$ to $-0.5$]; the second-layer position became shallower, but the death rate did not fall (Appendix D). Up to this point the results agree with the reading of Section 3.3: ELU, whose zero deepens with the width, is rescued when the width is stopped, and ReLU, which has no zero in a living state, does not reopen when the width is stopped. The exception is ELU in real-label 5+1, where cap12 was worse by 2.0 points. The registered prediction that the effect splits by activation alone fails here, and the cost of the cap to learning in a fresh network (0.06 points) cannot explain it (Appendix D).

### 4.4 Exchanging the response: the effect on relearning

**〔4.4-1〕§3.4の応答の喪失が学び直しを削るかを、分岐時の重み・活動・予測を保ったまま第2層の応答だけを入れ替えて調べる。**

We test whether the loss of response of Section 3.4 reduces relearning by exchanging only the second-layer response, while keeping the weights, the activities, and the predictions at the branch point. Let $z_i^0(x)$ be the preactivation at the branch point and $z_i^{\rm src}(x)$ the preactivation of the source network. We fix their difference $o_i(x)=z_i^{\rm src}(x)-z_i^0(x)$ and set the output after the branch to $\varphi(z_i^0)+\varphi(z_i+o_i)-\varphi(z_i^0+o_i)$. At the branch point, the activities, the logits, the loss, and the weights coincide with the control, and only the derivative used in training becomes $\varphi'(z_i^{\rm src})$. After the branch, learning of the weights and the features resumes, and the derivative moves with the preactivation as $\varphi'(z_i+o_i)$.

**〔4.4-2〕同じ軌道の早期と劣化後で応答を双方向に交換した（表）。**

We exchanged the response in both directions between the early and the degraded states of the same trajectory (Table 1). The restoring direction inherits the Adam history; the direction that hands a low response to the early network resets the history in both the control and the intervention. In all three settings, restoration raised the relearning of the next task and the reverse exchange lowered it, and all 10 seeds agreed in direction in every comparison.

**〔4.4-3〕成長制限が保った応答の寄与も、乱数ラベルMNISTのELUで調べた。**

We also examined the contribution of the response that the growth cap preserves, in random-label MNIST with ELU. In this setting, cap12 constrains the norms of the two components of the first layer, split along the input-mean direction and its orthogonal complement, together with the row norms of the second layer; it improved on the control in all 10 seeds (online accuracy over tasks 51 to 100 about 84%, seed average). At the end of task 10, we exchanged the second-layer response between the cap12 network and the control network, resetting the Adam history in all arms. Here the offset was not fixed but was updated to follow the natural continuation of the source network. Over the next task of 6,000 updates, handing the control's response to the cap12 network lowered the online accuracy from 80.8% to 19.7%, and handing the cap12 response to the control raised it from 16.9% to 84.6%. This is the effect of an artificial intervention that shows the contribution of the response-related function; it is not a strict mediation rate of the natural benefit of the cap.

**〔4.4-4〕応答復元の効果は、復元時の勾配ノルム比またはAdamの変位比に合わせた固定学習率では再現できず…**

The effect of restoring the response was not reproduced by a fixed learning rate matched to the gradient-norm ratio or to the Adam displacement ratio at restoration; both controls fell below restoration in all six comparisons of the three settings (Appendix E).

**〔4.4-5〕MLPでは、機構の四段がそれぞれの介入で確かめられた。**

In MLPs, each of the four stages of the mechanism was confirmed by its own intervention. Learning creates the push, the mean and bias pathways carry it, stopping the width stops the sinking of ELU, and restoring the response restores relearning. The next section verifies the same four stages in convolutional networks, where the input to a hidden layer moves with learning.
